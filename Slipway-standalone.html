How the physics works

Splitting the weight

On a ramp at angle θ the weight mg splits into mg sin θ pulling down the slope and mg cos θ pressing into it. The ramp pushes back with the normal force N = mg cos θ.

Will it slip?

Static friction can hold up to μs N. The block starts sliding when mg sin θ > μs mg cos θ, which simplifies to tan θ > μs. Mass and gravity cancel out.

Sliding down

Once moving, kinetic friction μk N acts up the slope, so a = g(sin θ − μk cos θ). If that is negative, the ramp slows the block down even though it is going downhill.

Speed and time

With constant acceleration, v² = v₀² + 2aL gives the speed at the bottom and t = (v − v₀)/a the time on the ramp.

Stopping on the bench

On the flat bench friction gives a = −μk g, so the block stops after d = v²/(2μk g). From rest this becomes d = L(sin θ − μk cos θ)/μk, with no g or m in it.

Energy

Potential energy mgh becomes kinetic energy ½mv², and friction converts it to heat at a rate of μk N joules per metre. The three always add up to the starting total.
